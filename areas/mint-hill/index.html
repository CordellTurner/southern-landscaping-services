Keep the useful parts of a wooded yard

Manage the leaves.
Make room for the lawn.

From the established center of Mint Hill near Veterans Memorial Park to larger wooded residential lots, yards can shift from sunny grass to tree cover in a few steps. That mix means uneven light, roots near planting beds, and a long season of falling leaves. Decide which areas should stay open, which should remain natural, and which need regular attention.

Ways SLS can help

  • Leaf removal for lawns and beds where leaf cover has built up.
  • Lawn care and maintenance for mowing, aeration, fertilizer application, and weed control.
  • Planting and bed maintenance for beds that need a new layout or ongoing care.
  • Mulch and pine needle installation to refresh established beds.

Think about access and water

When requesting an estimate, mention long driveways, gates, steep areas, and where a crew could access the work. If a low spot remains wet or water flows toward the house, describe it and note what happens after rain. Grading and drainage changes affect neighboring land and may require specialist assessment.

For planting under trees, consider the actual amount of sunlight and whether the roots make digging difficult. Avoid raising soil or piling mulch against tree trunks. For a patio or retaining wall, share the slope and discuss any permits or community rules before agreeing to construction.
