Shade, roots, and compact outdoor spaces

Make a city yard
easier to care for.

Around Freedom Park and the Little Sugar Creek Greenway, yards can be shaped by mature canopy, sidewalk edges, and a compact footprint. Grass and plantings may receive very different light only a few feet apart. Check sun, soil moisture, and root competition before replacing a struggling planting bed. Clear edges and a thoughtful layout can help smaller yards feel more orderly.

Services to consider

  • Planting and bed maintenance to refresh a shaded entry or a small side bed.
  • Leaf removal when fall coverage settles across lawn and paved areas.
  • Mulch or pine needle installation for an established garden bed.
  • Patios and retaining walls to discuss improving how you use the available outdoor space.

Keep the project proportional

In a small yard, new paving, planting, and storage all compete for the same space. Think about how people enter the yard, where water drains, and how much maintenance you want before deciding what to change. Tell us about stairs, gates, narrow paths, or parking that may affect access to the work.

If you are considering a wall or patio, confirm City, historic-area, and neighborhood requirements for your specific address. Share any approvals with the project team. Drainage problems should be evaluated before landscaping conceals their source.

Getting started

  1. Tell us the cross street or address near the park.
  2. Describe the specific beds, lawn, or outdoor area you want to improve.
  3. Talk through options, estimate, and schedule with the SLS team.
