A homeowner's guide to garden beds

How often should
landscaping mulch be replaced?

Don't go by the calendar alone. Look at how much material is left, whether weeds are getting through, and whether the bed needs a full refresh or just a light top-up.

Inspect the bed before scheduling

Mulch breaks down over time, and its color fades with sun and rain. Walk around the planting beds once or twice a year and look for bare soil, material mixed into the ground, weeds emerging through thin spots, or mulch that has collected too deeply. A refreshed surface is not always a reason to remove everything underneath.

Signs a refresh may help

  • The existing covering is thin or uneven and the soil is showing through.
  • Weeds are appearing in exposed areas where the material has washed or decomposed.
  • The beds look patchy next to the lawn, paths, or home.
  • The current material has shifted away from the areas it was meant to cover.

When a top-up is the better option

If the old layer has broken down and the bed is not already deep, adding a modest amount may be enough. Before adding material, pull obvious weeds, check that water can still reach the plants, and clear mulch away from stems and tree trunks. Don't keep stacking new layers on top of old ones without checking the total depth.

What affects the timing?

Exposure, rainfall, slopes, bed maintenance, and the material itself all affect how quickly a bed changes. A sunny edge may fade differently from a shaded bed beneath trees. Pine needles and shredded hardwood also settle and look different over time, so choose based on appearance, availability, and the bed's needs rather than assuming they follow the same schedule.

NC State Extension's landscape resources can help with general bed care and plant selection. For plant-specific concerns or signs of disease, consult a local nursery or Cooperative Extension office rather than hiding the issue beneath fresh mulch.

Should old mulch be removed every year?+

Not automatically. Check how much remains and whether the bed is already deep. A light refresh may be sufficient; avoid building a thick layer around trunks or plant crowns.

Does mulch stop every weed?+

No. Mulch can reduce exposed soil and make some weeds easier to manage, but it doesn't prevent all weeds, especially if weed seeds land on top of the material.

Can you install mulch without changing my existing plants?+

Point out which plants and bed areas should stay as they are. We'll discuss the installation scope with you before the work is scheduled.
