Planting guide for the Piedmont

Best landscaping plants
for Charlotte, NC.

There isn't one best plant for every Charlotte yard. Start with the conditions a plant will actually live in, then choose varieties that suit the space and the time you want to spend caring for it.

Four checks before you pick a plant

  1. Light: Track direct sun through the day. "Part shade" and deep shade are not interchangeable, and deciduous trees change the light by season.
  2. Soil and drainage: Notice whether the soil stays wet, drains quickly, or compacts. Piedmont yards can have clay-heavy soil, but conditions vary even within one property.
  3. Room to mature: Check a plant's mature width and height before planting near foundations, paths, windows, utility lines, and tree trunks.
  4. Maintenance: Consider pruning, seasonal cleanup, wildlife, and whether the plant needs regular watering while it establishes.

Examples to research for Charlotte gardens

These are starting points to investigate, not a universal planting list. Verify the specific plant and cultivar with a local nursery or NC State Extension plant database before buying.

  • Oakleaf hydrangea: A native shrub to research for part-shade locations with room to grow; check mature size and moisture needs.
  • Virginia sweetspire: A native flowering shrub to consider for a site that has suitable light and soil moisture. Confirm the variety's mature spread before using it in a narrow bed.
  • Eastern redbud: A small native tree option to evaluate where it has adequate space, light, and soil conditions as it matures.
  • Evergreen structure: For year-round screening, compare locally suitable evergreen plants by their mature height, width, light needs, and maintenance rather than selecting by appearance alone.

Use the NC State Extension Gardener Plant Toolbox to filter plants by site conditions and review mature size. The tool notes that "right plant, right place" is central to selecting something likely to thrive. If a tree canopy, shallow roots, or existing irrigation limits your options, assess those constraints first.

Common Charlotte planting mistakes to avoid

  • Planting full-sun species below dense tree canopy and expecting them to thrive.
  • Choosing a shrub based on its nursery size instead of its mature spread.
  • Installing plants in a low spot without considering where stormwater flows.
  • Covering tree trunks or plant stems with soil or a deep mulch pile.
  • Removing an established plant before checking whether it can be pruned or relocated.
