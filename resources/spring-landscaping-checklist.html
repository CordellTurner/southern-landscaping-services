Seasonal yard care

A Charlotte homeowner's
spring landscaping checklist.

Walk the property before booking a long list of treatments. Look for what changed over winter, what is growing well, and where the yard needs practical attention.

Ask about spring work ↗Read the fall checklist

A flexible checklist for Charlotte yards · Timing depends on weather, grass type, and site conditions.

Start with a slow walk around the property

  1. Look for drainage changes. Note soil washout, new puddles, blocked downspouts, or water collecting near the house. Resolve the source before covering it with mulch or planting.
  2. Check the lawn. Mark thin areas and note whether they are shaded, compacted, or bare from foot traffic. Identify the grass type before choosing aeration or fertilizer timing.
  3. Inspect beds. Look for broken branches, exposed soil, weeds, winter damage, and plants that may have outgrown their spots. Preserve plants you want to keep before scheduling cleanup.
  4. Review paths, patios, and walls. Notice shifting pavers, movement, or a leaning wall. Structural changes and water issues should be assessed rather than hidden by landscaping.

Plan planting before buying

Check how much direct sun each bed receives, whether the soil stays wet or dries quickly, and how much room plants have at mature size. Use the NC State Extension Plant Toolbox to compare plants for local conditions, then confirm selections for your site. Spring flowers on a nursery rack aren't proof a plant will thrive in the spot you have.

Refresh beds only where needed

Before ordering material, check the depth and condition of what's already in the bed. Clear weeds and debris, keep fresh mulch or pine needles away from stems and trunks, and avoid burying tree roots. See how to decide when mulch needs refreshing or compare pine needles and mulch.

Set a realistic lawn-care plan

Charlotte lawns may have tall fescue or warm-season turf. They have different aeration, fertilizing, and renovation windows. Consult the current NC State Extension calendar for the grass type rather than applying a product just because spring has arrived. Our Charlotte aeration guide explains the seasonal difference.

Decide which jobs you can group

  • Planting and bed cleanup can be discussed with a bed-maintenance request.
  • Ask about mulch or pine needle installation after the bed is ready.
  • Request lawn maintenance based on what is actually growing.

Make a short priority list and share the property location, a photo, and any access constraints with the team. For ongoing drainage issues or suspected disease, consult the appropriate professional before authorizing landscape changes.

Should I fertilize the whole lawn every spring?+

No single application plan suits every grass type or lawn. Follow current turf-specific guidance and base nutrient decisions on lawn needs and soil testing where appropriate.

Should I aerate in spring?+

That depends on grass type. NC State Extension recommends delaying tall fescue aeration until fall; warm-season grasses follow a different growing-season schedule.

When should I replace mulch?+

Inspect the bed first. A top-up may be enough if the old layer has broken down and the total depth is not excessive.
