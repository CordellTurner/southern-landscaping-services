Water-management basics

Drainage solutions
for Charlotte yards.

Standing water has a cause and a path. Before adding a drain, wall, or new planting bed, find out where the water comes from and where it can safely go.

General guidance only · Persistent water near a foundation calls for prompt professional assessment.

Observe the water before choosing a fix

Charlotte's Piedmont soil and rolling residential lots can affect how quickly rain soaks in or moves across a property. A soggy lawn, eroding soil, or water near a house does not point to one universal solution. Watch the site during and after rain and note whether the water is coming from a roof, neighboring slope, compacted area, irrigation leak, or a low point in the yard.

Check the obvious sources first

  • Look for clogged gutters or downspouts that discharge next to the foundation.
  • Notice whether the soil slopes toward the home or retains water in a low area.
  • Check for compacted or bare soil where runoff repeatedly follows the same route.
  • Rule out irrigation leaks or a change in the way water drains from hard surfaces.
  • Take photographs after rain and track how long the area stays wet.

Why quick fixes can create a second problem

Filling a low area, adding topsoil, or installing a pipe may move water onto a neighbor's property or toward a building. French drains and retaining walls need a suitable outlet and site-specific planning. Before digging, identify underground utility lines and check whether the work needs a permit or professional design. Don't cover signs of a foundation leak or soil movement with landscaping material.

When to call a drainage specialist

Get qualified advice when water enters the house, soil is washing away, a wall is leaning, the ground remains saturated, or the project needs a new outlet or grading plan. Severe or recurring problems may require a drainage contractor, engineer, or other licensed professional; planting and mulch won't correct the underlying issue.

Southern Landscaping Services lists patios and retaining walls among its services, but those services should not be treated as a blanket drainage repair. See patio and retaining wall project considerations and share the problem before requesting an estimate.

Prepare useful details

  1. Mark the wet area and where nearby downspouts discharge.
  2. Note what happens during steady rain and how long water remains.
  3. Photograph affected ground, walls, and nearby property edges.
  4. Ask who is responsible for assessing the issue and confirming a safe discharge point.

For City of Charlotte or Mecklenburg County requirements, check with the relevant local authority before changing drainage, grading, or stormwater flow.

Can mulch fix a soggy patch?+

No. Mulch may improve the appearance of a bed, but it does not fix the cause of standing water or poor drainage.

Can a retaining wall solve water flowing downhill?+

Not without evaluating grade, soil, drainage, and a safe outlet. A wall can change where water travels, so get site-specific professional advice.
