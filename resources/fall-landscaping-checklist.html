Seasonal yard care

A Charlotte homeowner's
fall landscaping checklist.

Make a plan for leaf season and cooler weather before the lawn and beds disappear under autumn debris. Start with the needs of your grass and the trees on your property.

A fall guide for Charlotte yards · Turf maintenance varies by grass type and current conditions.

Before leaves begin to pile up

  • Walk the trees and lawn. Note where leaves collect, which beds need cleanup, and any paths or drains that should stay clear.
  • Plan lawn work by grass type. Tall fescue and warm-season turf do not share the same fall schedule. Use a grass-specific calendar and avoid treating the entire yard as one turf.
  • Check for compacted areas. Fall is the recommended tall fescue aeration season in NC State Extension guidance. Warm-season lawns have different needs.
  • Look over garden beds. Remove fallen leaves that are smothering low plants, but keep healthy leaf litter where it is appropriate and won't block lawn or walkways.
  • Check mulch before adding more. See whether the existing layer needs a light top-up rather than a complete replacement. Keep new material away from trunks and plant stems.
  • Inspect hard surfaces. Look for loose pavers, trip hazards, leaning walls, and runoff issues before wet or cold weather makes them harder to address.

Manage leaves in stages

Large trees can drop leaves over several weeks, so a single cleanup may not fit every property. Decide which lawn, beds, entry paths, and outdoor areas you need kept clear. A thick layer left in place can block light and air, while clearing every leaf from natural areas is not always necessary. Match cleanup to how you use the space.

Southern Landscaping Services offers seasonal leaf removal. Include the specific areas and timing you want when asking about a visit. If leaves repeatedly clog a storm drain or a waterway, report it to the appropriate public agency instead of pushing debris into the street.

Fall lawn care in the Charlotte area

NC State Extension recommends fall aeration for tall fescue; it does not apply the same calendar to warm-season lawns. Identify the grass and review the current university recommendations before overseeding or fertilizing. Avoid applying a treatment based only on the season or the appearance of a brown patch.

Get beds ready for cooler weather

Remove diseased plant material carefully, note plants that have outgrown their place, and avoid drastic pruning without checking the plant's requirements. Refresh mulch only after checking the existing depth. For new plant choices, see plants suited to Charlotte conditions.

Is fall a good time to aerate?+

For tall fescue, NC State Extension recommends aerating in fall. Warm-season grass timing is different, so identify the turf first.

Do leaves need to be removed from all garden beds?+

Not necessarily. Clear leaves that smother plants, block paths, or build up where they cause problems; healthy leaf litter in suitable garden areas may be left in place.

How often should I schedule leaf cleanup?+

It depends on tree cover, weather, and how clear you want the lawn. Tell us which areas matter most and we'll discuss availability.
